The Saint

About Saint Seville

Behind the name of this site stands a real figure: Isidore of Seville, who lived from about 560 to 636. As archbishop he led the Church in Visigothic Spain for more than three decades, presided over councils that shaped the Church in his land, and was declared a Doctor of the Church in 1722. His feast day is April 4.

What makes Isidore of Seville beloved here is what he did with knowledge. Late antiquity was a time when learning was scattering and libraries were disappearing. Isidore of Seville answered by gathering. His great work, the Etymologiae, collected the knowledge of his age into twenty ordered books, from grammar and law to medicine, agriculture, and the nature of God. For centuries it sat on the shelf of nearly every serious library in Europe. He did not invent most of what it contains. He organized it, faithfully, so the next generation could find it.

Patron of an ordered internet

Because of that life spent arranging knowledge for others, Isidore of Seville has long been proposed as the patron saint of the internet, computer users, and programmers. A man who built a structured index of everything his world knew is a fitting intercessor for an age drowning in unstructured information.

Why we call him Saint Seville

Saint Seville is this project's familiar name for Isidore of Seville and for the instinct he represents: that knowledge is meant to be gathered, kept in order, and passed on faithfully. The site presents him as the patron saint of information organization. It is an affectionate title rather than a formal one, and everything offered under it follows his example. Nothing invented, everything sourced, all of it arranged so you can find your way to the original.

"In the spirit of Isidore of Seville: gather what is true, put it in order, and hand it on with its sources intact."

To learn what this site does with that spirit, see About SaintSeville.org. To see the documents it draws from, see the Sources page.
