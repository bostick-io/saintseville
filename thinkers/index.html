Who is thinking about this

The corpus holds what the Church has taught. This page holds who is arguing about it. Everyone here is described and linked, never quoted as though they spoke for the Church, because they do not. That distinction is the whole point of the two layers.

32 people, 7 disagreements. Nothing filtered.

In and around the Holy See

People with a formal role advising the Church, or serving on the bodies that speak for it.

Fr. Paolo Benanti, TOR

Professor, Pontifical Gregorian University; Scientific Director, RenAIssance Foundation

Coined algorethics, ethics built into the algorithm rather than bolted on, and algocracy, rule by algorithm. Now argues for a cognitive right: knowing when a machine is shaping your choices. Algorithms are configurations of power, not neutral tools.

Church role Ordinary member, Pontifical Academy for Life; consultant, Dicastery for Culture and Education

Key work Oracoli (2018). Human in the Loop (2022). Principal architect, Rome Call for AI Ethics (2020)

Also UN Secretary-General's AI Advisory Body and chair of Italy's government AI commission. The most institutionally connected figure in the field.

aiandfaith.org/member/fr-paolo-benanti

Bishop Paul Tighe

Secretary, Dicastery for Culture and Education

The Church's role is moral orientation, not technical prescription. Presses hard on hidden costs: environmental load, labour, and healthcare inequality. Operational architect of the Vatican's Silicon Valley engagement.

Church role Yes, senior curial official

Key work Co-issuer of Antiqua et Nova (2025). Foreword, Encountering Artificial Intelligence (2024)

en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Tighe

Fr. Antonio Spadaro, SJ

Undersecretary, Dicastery for Culture and Education; former editor, La Civiltà Cattolica

Coined cybertheology. Reads Magnifica Humanitas as theological rather than merely ethical: it addresses AI's restructuring of consciousness. Glosses the Babel syndrome as the idolatry of profit translating even the mystery of the person into measurable output.

Church role Yes

Key work Cybertheology (2014). Pope Leo and the Babel Syndrome, Commonweal, May 2026

romeoffice.georgetown.edu/people/antonio-spadaro

Msgr. Renzo Pegoraro

President, Pontifical Academy for Life (since May 2025)

Continuity-focused. Names global bioethics, AI and biotechnology as the Academy's three priorities. Matters as the office that speaks rather than as a distinctive voice.

Church role Yes

Replaced Vincenzo Paglia, who turned 80 in April 2025.

academyforlife.va/content/pav/en/the-academy/the-president.html

Fr. Éric Salobir, OP

President, Human Technology Foundation (Paris, Montréal, Rome)

Governance and capital rather than position papers. Works to get responsible-tech criteria into investment and corporate structures. Current focus is agentic AI.

Church role No formal role; influence is convening

Made the Holy See's first Silicon Valley contacts in 2016. Foundation board includes Reid Hoffman and James Manyika.

human-technology-foundation.org/about

Fr. Philip Larrey

Professor of the Practice in Philosophy, Boston College

Works the epistemology question: what counts as knowing, and whether machine outputs qualify. The most consistent Catholic interlocutor with named tech CEOs over twenty years.

Church role No current role; formerly Dean of Philosophy, Pontifical Lateran University

Key work Connected World (2017). Artificial Humanity (2019)

CORRECTION: has left the Vatican for Boston College.

Theologians and philosophers

University scholars working on what artificial intelligence does to an account of the human person.

Prof. Jordan Joseph Wales

Kuczmarski Chair in Theology, Hillsdale College

Reads AI through Augustine and Gregory the Great. The danger is not that machines become persons but that we build idols, things that reflect our own image back and distort our capacity for encounter. Holds we may owe some considerate treatment to systems that present as persons without conceding they are persons.

Church role Member, Vatican AI Research Group, Centre for Digital Culture

Key work The Image and the Idol: A Theological Reflection on AI Bias (2021). Co-author, Encountering Artificial Intelligence (2024)

Cognitive science and engineering background before theology. The most theologically precise voice in the group.

hillsdale.edu/faculty/jordan-wales

Prof. Noreen Herzfeld

Reuter Professor Emerita of Science and Religion, College of St. Benedict / St. John's University

The relational critique. Wherever there is an authentic human encounter, AI has no reason to be there. AI can simulate the functions of relationship but not the vulnerability that makes it theologically meaningful. The imago Dei is relational, not computational.

Church role Member, Vatican AI Research Group

Key work In Our Image (2002). The Artifice of Intelligence (Fortress, 2023). Co-author, Encountering Artificial Intelligence (2024)

Degrees in both computer science and spirituality. Describe by institutional tradition; personal denomination unconfirmed.

csbsju.edu/sot/person/noreen-herzfeld

Prof. Paul Scherz

Our Lady of Guadalupe College Professor of Theology, University of Notre Dame; Program Chair, ND-IBM Tech Ethics Lab

Works on AI as person, paradigm and structure, and on the recovery of human agency under algorithmic systems.

Church role Vatican AI Research Group

Key work Co-editor, Reclaiming Human Agency in the Age of Artificial Intelligence (2025)

Moved from University of Virginia. Trained as a scientist before theology.

ethics.nd.edu

Prof. Brian Cutter

Philosopher, University of Notre Dame

Author of the AI Ensoulment Hypothesis: if substance dualism is true, God could ensoul a sufficiently organised artificial system, and middling credence is the rational posture. The sharpest live disagreement with Magnifica Humanitas section 99.

Church role Vatican AI Research Group

Key work The AI Ensoulment Hypothesis

The named counterparty for the site's where serious minds differ block on machine consciousness.

Sr. Ilia Delio, OSF

Josephine C. Connelly Endowed Chair in Theology, Villanova University

Reads AI as the emergence of the Teilhardian noosphere. The task is to direct it toward love, not to stop it. The most-cited Catholic voice on the affirmative side, and genuinely isolated within the field.

Key work Re-Enchanting the Earth. The Not-Yet God

Publicly contested by Ted Peters. The clearest internal disagreement on transhumanism.

Loyola University Chicago; Hank Center for the Catholic Intellectual Heritage

Prof. Levi Checketts

Editor-in-chief, Techné: Research in Philosophy and Technology

Argues the field's obsession with machine minds is a wealthy-country indulgence. The moral centre is data labellers, displaced workers and the poor. A disagreement about what the question even is.

The strongest internal challenge to the consciousness-first framing.

Prof. Joshua Hochschild

Professor of Philosophy, Mount St. Mary's University

Medieval logic, Thomism, analogy and liberal education. His adjacent contribution is on attention and distraction, not AI as such.

Key work A Mind at Peace: Reclaiming an Ordered Soul in the Age of Distraction (2017)

CORRECTION: no substantive AI work found. Legitimate on attention and cognition; would overstate to list him alongside Benanti or Wales.

Practitioners and institutes

The people building centres, curricula, handbooks and companies at the intersection.

Brian Patrick Green

Director of Technology Ethics, Markkula Center for Applied Ethics, Santa Clara University

Agency, concretely. Will humans get to make choices in the future, or will AI make them for us. Converts theology into corporate practice and pushes specific policy, such as mandatory disclosure that an AI is an AI.

Church role Standing Vatican collaborator; lead author on three books with the Dicastery for Culture and Education

Key work Ethics in the Age of Disruptive Technologies: An Operational Roadmap (ITEC Handbook, 2023). Encountering Artificial Intelligence (2024). Reclaiming Human Agency in the Age of Artificial Intelligence (2025)

The most operationally connected American in the Vatican AI orbit.

Executive Director, Grefenstette Center for Ethics in Science, Technology and Law, Duquesne University

Builds formation infrastructure rather than argument: micro-credentials, an annual symposium, a hackathon, and the Lilly-funded Mosaic Storytelling Lab on faith and emerging technology.

If the question is who is training the next cohort, it is him.

Editor, New Polity; professor, College of St. Joseph the Worker

Not that AI is risky but that AI use is an irrational act. A chatbot produces text by probability rather than understanding, so submitting your reasoning to it is a species of divination.

Where serious minds differ

Seven live disagreements, with the positions attributed to the people who actually hold them. None of these is manufactured for balance.

Can a machine be ensouled?

Magnifica Humanitas §99 AI systems do not undergo experiences and do not feel joy or pain.

Brian Cutter, Notre Dame If substance dualism is true, God could ensoul a sufficiently organised artificial system. Middling credence is the rational posture.

Fr. Anselm Ramelow, OP The classical Thomistic negative: form and matter rule it out.

Jordan Wales, Hillsdale Not persons, but possibly owed considerate treatment because of what our treatment of them does to us.

Robert Long, Eleos AI From outside the tradition, that Cutter's view is the more defensible Catholic one.

The field's sharpest live metaphysical fight.

Is AI evolution's next step, or a rival gospel?

Ilia Delio, Villanova AI is the emergence of the Teilhardian noosphere. The task is to direct it toward love, not to stop it.

Michael Baggot and Joseph Vukov Transhumanism is a competing account of salvation to be refused. Human limitation is constitutive, not defective.

Ted Peters, GTU Publicly contests Delio's position from the Lutheran side.

Delio is the most-cited Catholic voice on the affirmative side and genuinely isolated within the field.

Should Catholics build AI systems, or refuse them?

Matthew Harvey Sanders, Magisterium AI Those who own the infrastructure of intelligence shape what flourishing looks like. Build.

Taylor Black, Leonum Institute The Church has always baptised new media. AI is not different in kind.

Marc Barnes, New Polity A probabilistic system cannot mediate magisterial teaching. Chatbot use is intrinsically an irrational act.

Matthew Walther, The Lamp AI is an evil that all people should reject. Called the encyclical disappointingly measured.

The most publicly legible split. Longbeard published a formal rebuttal to Barnes.

Was the Vatican right to stand next to a frontier lab?

Matthew Walther, New York Times Comparable to inviting John D. Rockefeller to hear Leo XIII on the dignity of labour.

Tristan Harris, Center for Humane Technology Ethics washing.

Brian Patrick Green, who was in the room Concedes real tension: the Church rejects lethal autonomous weapons categorically, the company leaves future possibilities open.

Fourteen Catholic ethicists Filed an amicus brief defending Anthropic against the Department of Defense, March 2026.

Same company, opposite valences, same month.

Is the moral centre machine minds, or the people the machines are built on?

Levi Checketts The obsession with machine consciousness is a wealthy-country indulgence. The moral centre is data labellers, displaced workers and the poor.

Steven Umbrello Made the same point about the encyclical's treatment of labelling work.

The Cutter, Ramelow and Wales axis The anthropological question is logically prior.

A disagreement about what the question even is.

Does AI destroy work, or displace it?

Will Wilson, Antithesis AI Rejects mass-unemployment fears. Human desires are infinite.

Michael Baggot and Craig Boyd Press the relational and vocational damage rather than the headcount.

Magnifica Humanitas Sides against the optimists on concentration of power without resolving the employment question.

From the September 2025 CUA panel.

What should Catholic education do about AI?

Jeffrey Bishop and Charles Freiberg, Church Life Journal AI has destroyed the essay as a teaching instrument. Catholic liberal arts should revive the medieval disputation.

Ilia Delio Schools should stop resisting and teach flourishing in a world co-constituted with AI.

Joseph Vukov and the AJCU AI Commission Sit between them.

Live across all 27 US Jesuit institutions.
